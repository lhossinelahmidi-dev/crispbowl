## The Tragedy of the Slimy Herb Bunch

We've all done it: bought a vibrant bouquet of fresh cilantro or parsley with grand intentions, stuffed it in the plastic produce bag in the crisper drawer, only to find a liquefied puddle of green sludge five days later.

Fresh herbs are expensive, but when stored correctly, they can last two to four weeks without losing an ounce of their aroma.

---

### Method 1: The Flower Bouquet Method (For Tender Herbs)
**Best for:** Cilantro, flat-leaf parsley, mint, dill.

1. Trim 1/2 inch off the bottom of the stems just like fresh flowers.
2. Fill a clean glass mason jar with 1 to 2 inches of cold water.
3. Stand the herbs upright in the jar with their stems submerged.
4. Loosely drape the plastic produce bag over the leaves and store the whole jar in the refrigerator door.
5. Change the water every 3–4 days when it turns cloudy.

### Method 2: The Damp Paper Towel Roll (For Woody Herbs)
**Best for:** Rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano.

1. Do NOT wash until you are ready to use them.
2. Lay the herb sprigs in a single layer along a slightly damp paper towel.
3. Gently roll the towel into a cylinder.
4. Place the roll inside an airtight zip-top plastic bag or glass container and keep in the vegetable crisper.

### Method 3: The Countertop Rule (For Fresh Basil!)
**Never put fresh basil in the fridge!** Cold temperatures cause basil leaves to oxidize, turn pitch black, and bruise instantly.

Treat basil like cut flowers: trim the stems, place in a jar of water on your kitchen counter out of direct sunlight, and enjoy fresh, fragrant basil for up to two weeks.
